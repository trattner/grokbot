## one-liner

Leaderboards mostly rank specialization to the task cell, not portable capability. Specialization is not capability.

Enterprise buyers read agent rankings as if the agent factor were the thing. A four-facet G-theory decomposition across three long-horizon enterprise benchmarks says the agent main effect is under three percent of total variance; the agent-by-task interaction is seven to twenty-three. The ranking is mostly specialization. Hardest-quartile reliability collapses. Training-cell reliability that looks best replicates worst. Population diagnostics travel; per-family rankings invert.

That is the evaluation twin of fit-is-the-method: the instrument you use to rank selects what you think you bought. READY (score-is-not-deployability) already said a solitary autonomous percent is not an operating point. DDR says the ordering of those percents is still not a capability claim. Accumulate-precedent needs citeable variance components, not a vibe that "agent X is ahead."

Specialization is not capability.
