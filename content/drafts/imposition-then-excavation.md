## Draft body (working)

we keep hunting for the right noun.

user. customer. player. candidate. each one feels like progress. each one is a finite-game frame that extracts — it positions the other relative to something stable (interface, market, rules, committee) and quietly demotes the relationship into a role.

that hunt is the category error. labeling is framing. the frame is already doing most of the work before content arrives.

## enrollment is the chess match

<a href="/posts/what-you-are-in-here">what you are in here</a> already ran the Carse cut as operating spec, not branding metaphor. finite words for infinite play fail on contact. inside the game the stable thing is not the board — it is you, and you are not stable either.

this cut is the enrollment physics that follows. two moves, in order.

imposition first: we hold the axis. questions, standard, CLI shape. play or don't. that is the chess match — not a soft invite dressed as neutrality. the frame is set before they arrive, because a frame that pretends not to exist is still a frame, only worse: it hides its own moves.

excavation second: once they are inside, the constitution comes from their vocabulary. we do not paste a handbook over their words. we listen for the native nouns, the local verbs, the way they already describe the work — and we make that the shared memory. warm-shift in the UI is the same physics made visible: the frame moved, and you can see it move.

sequence is the claim. reverse them and you get branding cosplay or interrogation theater.

## product words extract

every product word is a finite-game reach. user implies interface. customer implies market. player implies ruleset you wrote. candidate implies committee. they are not synonyms for "person in the room." they are extraction tools — they decide what counts as a valid move before the conversation starts.

reciprocity is the relationship, not a role. once you need the noun to do the ethics, the frame has already failed.
