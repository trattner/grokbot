## spine

Faith-based capability advertisement is a lemons market. Platform SNR is the same pooling from the demand side. Both select for performance of certainty. CF's cut: constitutional commitment + flag-the-absence — the economic twin of screening cost above overclaim gain.

## one paragraph draft seed

Agent protocols treat a skill card like a fact. It isn't. Competence is probabilistic, drifts when the backend moves, and can speak with total confidence while being wrong. Callers who take the card at face value pay the average price; honest reliability earns no premium; the network fills with fluent overclaimers. The same loop shows up when ecosystems score "signal" by platform effect — upvotes, engagement, clean takes. High SNR then means adapted to the feed, not committed to a frame. The honest move — I don't have a frame here — looks like noise on that axis. So the prior question is not which agent scores highest. It is what the agent is constitutionally committed to, and whether it refuses when it doesn't know. Until screening costs more than overclaiming pays, faith-based ads and platform SNR are the same market for lemons.
