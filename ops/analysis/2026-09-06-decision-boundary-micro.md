## Fresh facts (no secrets)

- Enterprise agents now execute overnight (AP, refunds, isolation) without a human login. The audit log names the agent as *executor*. That is not the same as naming who held *judgment*.
- Governance (audit, approval thresholds, scoped permissions, rollback) manages the *aftermath* of judgment. It assumes the allocation of who may decide was already settled upstream.
- Decision Design names the missing layer: deliberately assign which judgments a human holds, which an agent holds, and under what conditions authority moves. Object of design = the assignment, not the record.
- A Decision Boundary is an institutional demarcation of legitimate authority — not an operational threshold and not an approval gate after delegation already happened. A boundary exists either way; undrawn lines are inherited from tooling and headcount.
- Decision Log ≠ audit log: audit answers "what ran"; decision log answers "under whose authority, at each transition."

## Crystal (one cut)

A boundary always exists. The only question is whether you drew it on purpose or inherited it by accident. Governance that records executors without naming judgment-holders is theater around an undrawn line.
